## Purpose

Establish the size and structure of the addressable market for ChronoSentiment as a Financial Decision Intelligence Platform. Identify the most viable initial customer segments and understand their technology adoption patterns. Provide the market evidence base for the PRD commercial assumptions.

---

## 1. Evidence

### 1.1 Global Investment Management Industry Size

Global assets under management reached approximately **US$139 trillion in 2024**, with forecasts reaching **US$200 trillion by 2030** at a 6.2% CAGR. (PwC Global Asset & Wealth Management Report, 2025) **Confidence: A**

Despite AuM growth, approximately **89% of asset managers report profitability pressure**, driven by fee compression, rising operational costs, and increasing client expectations. This is driving investment in AI-enabled operating models and workflow technology. (PwC, 2025) **Confidence: A**

Private markets are expected to account for more than half of global asset management industry revenues by 2030, expanding the addressable market beyond traditional public equity managers. (PwC, 2025) **Confidence: B**

### 1.2 Market Segmentation

| Segment | AUM (est. 2024) | Number of firms (est.) | Confidence |
|---------|----------------|----------------------|-----------|
| Large asset managers (>$50B AUM) | ~$70T | ~200 | B |
| Mid-size asset managers ($1B–$50B) | ~$25T | ~2,000 | B |
| Small asset managers ($100M–$1B) | ~$6T | ~10,000 | C |
| Hedge funds (all sizes) | ~$4T | ~8,000–10,000 | B |
| Family offices (>$100M) | ~$8T | ~8,000+ | A |
| Endowments and foundations | ~$2T | ~3,000–5,000 | C |

**Indicative TAM:** 30,000–40,000 organisations globally with structured investment decision processes. (Derived from segmentation estimates; not a precisely measured figure.) **Confidence: C**

**Indicative SAM:** Mid-size investment firms ($500M–$10B AUM) with 5–50 investment professionals — approximately 3,000–5,000 firms globally. **Confidence: C**

**Indicative SOM (3-year):** 50–200 firms, representing $3M–$12M ARR at proposed pricing. **Confidence: D**

### 1.3 Family Office Market

More than **8,000 family offices** globally, with continued growth. (Bloomberg Professional, 2025) **Confidence: A**

BlackRock's 2025 Global Family Office Survey (175 family offices, 27 markets, ~US$300B AUM collectively) identifies governance and technology as strategic priorities. **Confidence: A**

UBS's 2025 Global Family Office Report (317 single-family offices, average US$1.1B AUM) highlights increasing institutional sophistication, active portfolio management, and geopolitical risk management as top concerns. **Confidence: A**

### 1.4 Technology Adoption Trends

Asset managers increasingly view AI not only as an investment tool but as a **strategic operating capability**. PwC's 2025 report identifies technology, AI, and digital ecosystems as central to future competitiveness. **Confidence: A**

AI adoption is accelerating across asset management, driven by productivity pressure, client expectations, and cost reduction. Firms are seeking workflow integration rather than isolated point solutions. (PwC, 2025) **Confidence: A**

Family offices and private-market investors continue to grow in influence and sophistication, expanding the addressable market beyond traditional asset managers. (UBS, BlackRock, 2025) **Confidence: A**

Mid-size investment firms are actively replacing legacy workflows (email, spreadsheets, shared drives) with structured SaaS tools. The window for new entrants is open. **Confidence: B**

## Research Limitations

This document establishes market structure and industry trends through secondary sources. It does not establish:

- Willingness to purchase ChronoSentiment or any decision governance platform
- Buying processes, procurement cycles, or budget ownership within target segments
- Whether the identified customer segments experience the governance problems as purchasing priorities
- Product-market fit for ChronoSentiment's specific value proposition

Those questions require Phase 1B customer discovery. The market evidence here provides the foundation for Phase 1B hypothesis formation, not a substitute for primary research.

## 2. Research Findings

**Finding 1:** The global investment management market is large (US$139T AUM, 30,000–40,000 firms) and growing, with profitability pressure driving technology investment. **Confidence: A**

**Finding 2:** Mid-size active asset managers ($500M–$5B AUM) are the most viable initial segment — they have the pain, the budget, and the technology appetite, but have not yet been served by a purpose-built decision intelligence platform. **Confidence: B**

**Finding 3:** The family office segment is growing rapidly and professionalising, making it a credible secondary segment. **Confidence: A**

**Finding 4:** AI is becoming a strategic operating capability, not just an investment tool. Future differentiation will depend not only on better investment models but also on better governed investment workflows. **Confidence: A**

**Finding 5:** The market is transitioning from competing on market data access toward competing on investment decision quality and process discipline. **Confidence: B**

---

## 3. Implications

The addressable market is real, large, and growing. Regulatory pressure, AI governance requirements, and digital transformation in mid-market investment firms are creating demand for structured decision intelligence tools.

The profitability pressure finding is particularly important: 89% of asset managers under profitability pressure means technology that demonstrably improves decision quality or reduces operational cost has a clear budget justification.

The family office segment is growing faster than traditional asset management and is actively seeking institutional-grade tools — making it a credible secondary target that should not be deprioritised.

---

## 4. Recommendations

**R1:** Target mid-size active asset managers ($500M–$5B AUM) as the primary initial segment. This segment has the pain, the budget, and the technology appetite. **Confidence: B**

**R2:** Include family offices ($500M–$5B AUM) as a secondary segment in early commercial conversations. Their governance and documentation needs align closely with ChronoSentiment's value proposition. **Confidence: B**

**R3:** Position ChronoSentiment as a response to the AI governance and workflow digitisation trends, not as a data platform. The "better governed investment workflows" framing is more durable than a feature-comparison approach. **Confidence: D** (strategic interpretation)

**R4 — Why now?** The convergence of four trends creates a window: (a) AI adoption accelerating across investment firms; (b) profitability pressure driving technology investment; (c) governance requirements increasing from regulators and LPs; (d) workflow digitisation replacing legacy email/spreadsheet processes. This window is open now and will narrow as established vendors respond. **Confidence: B**

## 5. Customer Segment Profiles

### Segment A — Mid-size active asset managers (Primary)

**Profile:** $500M–$5B AUM, 5–20 investment professionals, active equity or multi-asset strategies.

**Pain points:** Investment decisions documented in emails and meeting notes. LP reporting requires manual reconstruction of decision rationale. Regulatory pressure to demonstrate investment process discipline. Knowledge retention risk when senior analysts leave.

**Technology posture:** Use Bloomberg Terminal, FactSet, or Refinitiv for data. May use a research management system (AlphaSense, Sentieo). Unlikely to have proprietary decision intelligence tooling.

**Buying behaviour:** Annual subscription, evaluated by CIO or Head of Research. Decision cycle 3–6 months.

**Willingness to pay:** $30,000–$80,000/yr (estimate; requires Phase 1B validation). **Confidence: C**

---

### Segment B — Hedge funds (Secondary)

**Profile:** $200M–$2B AUM, systematic or discretionary, 3–15 investment professionals.

**Pain points:** Execution validation. Investor due diligence requires documented investment process. Regulatory pressure increasing.

**Technology posture:** More technically sophisticated. May have proprietary backtesting infrastructure.

**Buying behaviour:** Faster decision cycle (1–3 months). More likely to evaluate on technical merit.

**Willingness to pay:** $40,000–$120,000/yr (estimate). **Confidence: C**

---

### Segment C — Family offices (Secondary)

**Profile:** $500M–$5B AUM, 3–10 investment professionals, multi-asset.

**Updated context (2025):** More than 8,000 family offices globally, rapidly professionalising. BlackRock and UBS surveys confirm governance and technology as strategic priorities.

**Pain points:** Governance and documentation for family principals and external advisors. Explainability of investment decisions to non-professional stakeholders. Knowledge retention.

**Willingness to pay:** $20,000–$60,000/yr (estimate). **Confidence: C**

---

### Segment D — Endowments and foundations (Tertiary)

**Profile:** $200M–$5B AUM, 2–8 investment professionals, long-horizon multi-asset.

**Pain points:** Board and investment committee governance requirements. Audit trail for investment decisions. Explainability to non-professional board members.

**Buying behaviour:** Long decision cycle (6–12 months). Procurement-driven.

**Willingness to pay:** $20,000–$50,000/yr (estimate). **Confidence: C**
