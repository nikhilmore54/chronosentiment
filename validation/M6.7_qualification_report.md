## 1. Qualification Objectives

This report synthesises the evidence collected across Milestones M6.1 through M6.6 of the Coralys Validation Programme. It provides a single qualification narrative for the `coralys-scheduling` optimizer, suitable for review by a technical stakeholder who has not read the individual milestone reports.

The qualification programme was designed to answer six independent engineering questions, each establishing a different property of the optimizer:

| Milestone | Engineering Question                                                     |
|-----------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| M6.1      | Does the codebase satisfy its architectural invariants?                  |
| M6.2      | Does the optimizer produce functionally correct solutions?               |
| M6.3      | Does the optimizer improve solution quality relative to a known baseline? |
| M6.4      | Does the optimizer scale to larger problem instances?                    |
| M6.5      | Does the optimizer remain correct and improve quality under disruption?  |
| M6.6      | How does the optimizer perform on representative scheduling instances?   |

Each milestone establishes a different property. None duplicate evidence from another. The programme progresses from intrinsic properties (M6.1–M6.5: correctness, quality, scalability, robustness) to an extrinsic property (M6.6: performance on representative instances).

## 2. Scope and Assumptions

### In Scope

- `LocalSearch` with `WorkloadBalanceObjective` (variance of total flight-leg counts per rotation)
- Swap and relocate neighborhood moves
- Feasible-state search (only moves producing immediately legal rosters are accepted)
- Synthetic qualification fixtures of up to 16 rotations / 64 pairings
- Single-objective optimisation

### Assumptions

- All qualification fixtures are deterministic and self-contained (no external dataset dependencies).
- The `LegalityChecker` used in each test reflects the legality rules relevant to that scenario. Tests using a bare checker (no rules registered) validate optimizer mechanics independently of domain rules.
- Fixture design follows two rules established in M6.3: (1) heavy rotations hold the latest pairings; (2) all rotations have ≥ 2 pairings. These rules ensure that the `relocate_pairing` neighborhood move can always execute and that chronological ordering constraints are satisfied.

### Out of Scope

- Multi-objective optimisation at scale
- Infeasible-state repair (the optimizer cannot recover from an illegal post-disruption baseline)
- Real airline scheduling datasets
- Hardware-dependent runtime guarantees

## 7. Robustness (M6.5)

**Commit:** `c496f6ba`

Three disruption scenarios were tested, covering three independent robustness dimensions:

| Scenario | Disruption Type | Baseline Score | Optimized Score | Improvement | Result |
|----------|-----------------|---------------|-----------------|-------------|--------|
| ROB-1    | Resource (crew unavailability) | 32.0000 | 0.0000 | 100.0% | PASS |
| ROB-2    | Demand (pairing cancellation)  | 6.7500  | 0.7500 | 88.9%  | PASS |
| ROB-3    | Policy (fatigue regulation)    | 4.0000  | 0.0000 | 100.0% | PASS |

ROB-2 achieved 88.9% improvement, which is the analytically minimal variance for 11 pairings across 4 rotations (perfect workload balance is mathematically impossible because 11 pairings cannot be distributed equally across four rotations; the minimum attainable variance is 0.75).

**Architectural finding:** During fixture design for ROB-3, it was established that `LocalSearch` is a feasible-state search: it only accepts moves that produce an immediately legal roster and cannot navigate through illegal intermediate states. The optimizer's robustness guarantee applies within its feasible operating envelope — the post-disruption baseline must itself be legal under the active legality checker. This is a deliberate architectural characteristic, not a defect. The finding is consistent with the M6.1 architectural invariant gate, which enforces that the optimizer never produces an illegal roster.

**Open item:** BM-3 did not reach the theoretical optimum within the current search configuration (residual variance 0.4375, 99.8% improvement). The contributing factors — finite iteration budget, neighbourhood structure, or local optimum — are not uniquely determined by the available evidence. This is not a correctness failure; all CI gates are satisfied.

## 9. Consolidated Findings

The following findings emerged across the qualification programme and are relevant to understanding the optimizer's behaviour:

**F-1: Chronological ordering constraint (M6.3).** `Rotation::new` validates that pairings are in chronological order. The `relocate_pairing` move appends to the end of the destination rotation. Fixtures must be designed so that relocated pairings are always later than existing pairings in the destination rotation.

**F-2: Minimum rotation size (M6.3).** `relocate_pairing` returns `None` if the source rotation has only one pairing. All rotations must have ≥ 2 pairings to ensure the optimizer can always find candidate moves.

**F-3: Acceptance ratio decreases with scale (M6.4, M6.6).** The fraction of candidate moves that improve the objective decreases as problem size grows. This is a characteristic property of steepest-descent local search and does not indicate a defect.

**F-4: Feasible-state search scope (M6.5).** The optimizer cannot repair an illegal roster. When a disruption renders the existing roster illegal, a separate repair or re-planning step is required before the optimizer can be invoked. This is a deliberate architectural property.

**F-5: Objective measures leg-count variance (M6.6).** `WorkloadBalanceObjective` measures variance of total flight-leg counts per rotation, not pairing counts. Pairings with more legs contribute proportionally more to the objective. This is the intended behaviour.

**F-6: Large-scale convergence (M6.6).** At 16 rotations / 64 pairings, the optimizer did not reach the theoretical optimum within the current search configuration. The contributing factors are not uniquely determined. Future investigation may consider increasing the iteration budget or extending the neighbourhood structure.

## 11. Qualified Operating Envelope

The qualification programme has implicitly defined the operating envelope within which the optimizer is validated. This section makes it explicit.

The optimizer is qualified to operate under the following conditions:

**Input:**
- A `Roster` that is legal under the active `LegalityChecker` at the time of invocation.
- At least two rotations, each with at least two pairings.
- Pairings within each rotation in chronological order (enforced by `Rotation::new`).

**Objective:**
- `WorkloadBalanceObjective` (variance of total flight-leg counts per rotation).
- Single-objective optimisation. Multi-objective weighting is supported but not benchmarked at scale.

**Search:**
- Steepest-descent local search with swap and relocate neighborhood moves.
- Feasible-state search: only moves producing immediately legal rosters are accepted.
- Iteration budget scaled with problem size (validated up to 16 rotations / 64 pairings).

**Guarantees within this envelope:**
- The optimized roster is legal under the active `LegalityChecker`.
- Total pairings are conserved.
- When an improving legal neighbour exists, the optimized objective score is strictly less than the baseline score for the qualification fixtures.

**Outside this envelope:**
- Illegal starting rosters: the optimizer will make zero improvements (all moves from an illegal state produce illegal states or are rejected).
- Very large instances: convergence to the theoretical optimum is not guaranteed within a fixed iteration budget.
- Infeasible-state repair: not supported.
