---

## Fixture Design

Applies the two design rules established in M6.3:

1. **Chronological ordering**: Heavy rotation holds the LATEST pairings; light
   rotations hold the EARLIEST. Ensures `relocate_pairing` moves satisfy the
   `Rotation::new` chronological ordering constraint.

2. **Minimum rotation size**: All rotations have ≥ 2 pairings. `Roster` stores
   rotations in `HashMap<CrewId, Rotation>` — iteration order is non-deterministic.
   Rotations with 1 pairing cannot donate to `relocate_pairing`.

## Scalability Analysis

### Evaluation count vs problem size

| Pairings | Evaluations | Ratio to previous |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| 12       | 105         | —                 |
| 24       | 977         | ×9.3              |
| 48       | 8350        | ×8.5              |

The observed evaluation counts increase approximately quadratically with problem
size, consistent with the expected O(rotations × pairings) neighborhood explored
by the current steepest-descent local search implementation.

### Acceptance ratio

| Scale  | Evaluations | Improvements | Acceptance ratio |
|--------|-------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Small  | 105         | 3            | 2.86%           |
| Medium | 977         | 7            | 0.72%           |
| Large  | 8350        | 15           | 0.18%           |

The acceptance ratio decreases from 2.86% to 0.18% as problem size increases,
indicating that improving neighbors become progressively sparser relative to the
total neighborhood. This behaviour is characteristic of steepest-descent local
search approaching an optimum on larger search spaces.

### Improvement quality

The optimizer reaches the analytically known optimum for each qualification fixture
(variance = 0.0) at all three scales. This is consistent with M6.3 findings — the
fixture is designed so the global optimum is reachable by the relocate-pairing
neighborhood.

## Observations for M6.7 Qualification Report

### Neighborhood growth

The O(rotations × pairings) neighborhood size of the current local search means
evaluation counts grow approximately quadratically with problem size. For
production-scale instances (hundreds of crew, thousands of pairings), a more
efficient neighborhood traversal strategy may be warranted. This is an
implementation refinement, not a correctness issue.

### Acceptance ratio trend

The decreasing acceptance ratio suggests that larger instances have sparser
improving regions. Future optimisation research may investigate alternative
neighbourhood exploration or diversification strategies (for example,
perturbation-based search or metaheuristics) if larger benchmark instances
demonstrate diminishing returns from the current steepest-descent approach.

### HashMap traversal order

The current implementation derives neighbourhood traversal order from `HashMap`
iteration. While this does not affect correctness, a deterministic traversal order
(for example, by sorting crew identifiers prior to exploration) would improve
reproducibility across implementations and execution environments.
