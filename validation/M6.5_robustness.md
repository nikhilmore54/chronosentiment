## CI Gates

For every disruption scenario, the following correctness invariants are enforced:

1. **Objective improvement:** `optimized_score < baseline_score`
2. **Pairing conservation:** total pairings in optimized roster equals total in post-disruption baseline
3. **Legality:** optimized roster is legal under the disruption's checker

All three gates pass for all three scenarios.

## Architectural Finding — Feasible-State Local Search

During fixture design for ROB-3, an important architectural property of the current `LocalSearch` implementation was identified and documented.

### Observation

An earlier ROB-3 design used a baseline of (6, 2, 2, 2) under a `max_pairings = 3` policy. In this configuration, the baseline itself is illegal (the heavy rotation has 6 pairings, exceeding the limit of 3). The optimizer made zero improvements: every candidate move from the 6-pairing rotation to a 2-pairing rotation produces a 5-pairing rotation, which still violates the policy. The optimizer correctly rejected all such moves, leaving the baseline unchanged.

### Root Cause

`LocalSearch` is a **feasible-state search**: it only accepts moves that produce an immediately legal roster. It cannot navigate through illegal intermediate states to reach a legal solution. This is a deliberate architectural characteristic, not a defect — the optimizer is not a repair heuristic.

### Implication

The optimizer's robustness guarantee applies within its feasible operating envelope: the post-disruption baseline must itself be legal under the active legality checker. When a disruption renders the existing roster illegal (e.g. a policy change that retroactively violates existing assignments), a separate repair or re-planning step is required before the optimizer can be invoked.

### Design Decision

The ROB-3 fixture was redesigned to use a (4, 4, 2, 2) baseline under a `max_pairings = 4` policy. This correctly models the operational scenario where a new policy is introduced and the current roster is immediately restructured to comply, after which the optimizer is used to improve balance within the compliant space. `LocalSearch` was not modified to support infeasible intermediate states; the feasible-state property is an architectural invariant.

This finding is consistent with the M6.1 architectural invariant gate, which enforces that the optimizer never produces an illegal roster.

## Fixture Design Rules (from M6.3)

The following rules, established during M6.3 fixture design, were applied throughout M6.5:

1. **Heavy rotation holds LATEST pairings; light rotations hold EARLIEST.** This ensures that relocating a pairing from a heavy rotation to a light rotation appends a later pairing after earlier ones, satisfying `Rotation::new`'s chronological ordering constraint.
2. **All rotations have ≥ 2 pairings.** `relocate_pairing` returns `None` if the source rotation has only 1 pairing (it cannot be left empty). Rotations with ≥ 2 pairings can always donate.
