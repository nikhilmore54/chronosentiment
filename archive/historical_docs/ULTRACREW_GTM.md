## The Problem

Workforce planning has become one of the largest operational costs for many organisations. Yet most scheduling systems stop after producing a roster. Planners are left to evaluate alternatives manually, respond to disruptions reactively, and justify decisions without supporting evidence.

The result is higher labour costs, inconsistent planning, compliance risk, and unnecessary operational disruption.

---

## Who UltraCrew Is For

UltraCrew is designed for organisations where workforce planning has a direct impact on operational performance, cost, and customer service.

It is particularly valuable for organisations that:

- Schedule hundreds or thousands of employees
- Operate across multiple locations or departments
- Manage complex skills, certifications, or compliance requirements
- Experience frequent operational disruptions
- Need to balance labour cost, service quality, and workforce fairness

---

## What UltraCrew Does Differently

UltraCrew is not simply a workforce scheduling application.

It continues where scheduling systems stop — helping planners evaluate alternatives, understand trade-offs, recover from disruptions, monitor operational performance, and continuously improve workforce decisions.

The difference is decision support, not just schedule generation.

## Pilot Scope

A typical UltraCrew pilot covers:

1. **Discovery** — understand the customer's planning process, constraints, and success criteria. Success metrics are agreed jointly at this stage.
2. **Data onboarding** — workforce data, contracts, demand patterns, historical schedules
3. **Configuration** — constraint rules, objectives, integration points
4. **Parallel Validation** — compare UltraCrew schedules against current process on historical data, running both in parallel
5. **Live operation** — run UltraCrew alongside the current process for one planning cycle
6. **Assessment** — measure outcomes against the success criteria agreed in Discovery

Typical pilot duration: 6–8 weeks.

---

## Success Criteria

Pilot success metrics are defined jointly during Discovery to reflect the customer's operational priorities. This ensures the pilot measures what matters to the customer, not what is convenient to measure. Typical targets vary by organisation and planning environment.

Typical measures include:

- Schedule generation time (target: reduction from hours to minutes, depending on planning process complexity)
- Constraint violation rate (target: zero hard constraint violations)
- Planner acceptance rate (target: >80% of generated schedules accepted without manual revision)
- Cost variance (target: measurable reduction in overtime or agency spend)
- Disruption recovery time (target: reduction in time to produce a valid recovery schedule)
